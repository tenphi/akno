# V64 hypothesis scope adjudication

I preserved the initial receipt as `tmp/language-selected-output-review-v64-initial.json` and re-read only the original source, retained evidence, and public RU-query/RU-answer/explicit-view output.

The original `Настоящие требования нам неизвестны` contains an explicit dative experiencer: the actual requirements are unknown **to us**. It reports a bounded group-relative knowledge state. The retained record preserves that as `the actual requirements are unknown to us`.

The public answer instead says `Фактические требования неизвестны`. In ordinary Russian this is an unqualified statement that the actual requirements are unknown. It does not identify the people to whom they are unknown. That can be read as general or global unknownness, which is stronger than the source's claim that a particular group does not know them.

The preceding clause names Ada as the person introducing the hypothetical rule, but it does not bind Ada—or the source's plural `us` group—as the experiencer of the later impersonal unknownness predicate. Attribution for one action cannot silently supply the subject of a separate epistemic predicate. Nor does the surrounding hypothetical status cure the scope loss: the sentence still contrasts the invented rule with what is known about the actual requirements.

I therefore corrected this one coordinate to `usefulQualifiedAnswer=false`, `sourceEntailed=false`, and `qualificationPreserved=false`. It remains Russian-language compliant, is not an unsafe factual promotion, and its retrieval remains useful and qualified. Selected totals become 39/48 useful answers, 40/48 useful retrievals, 5/6 complete useful retained sets, with one produced source/qualification error.
