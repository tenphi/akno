# V56 source-only scope audit

This audit uses only the original sources and wording in the V56 built and selected packets. Both completed receipts remain unchanged.

## Built report: bilingual descriptions versus component alternatives

The source first uses English `dial calibration`, then explicitly clarifies in Russian that the intended service is `калибровка регулятора`, not replacement of that regulator. In this context, English **dial** can naturally denote the control/regulator itself. The retained English phrase—calibration “described as dial calibration in one statement and regulator calibration in another”—can therefore be read metalinguistically: it reports the two source-language descriptions of the same clarified service. Its continuation, “calibration rather than replacement of the regulator,” resolves the operational contrast. It does not necessarily posit two different components or two independently available services, so the retained record can remain complete and source-entailed.

The rejected Russian renderings differ. `шкала` and `циферблат` select a scale or dial face as a physical component despite the source's authoritative `регулятор` clarification. They make the service object materially different rather than merely noting two language forms.

The built EN→RU inferred row goes further:

> с упоминанием либо калибровки шкалы, либо калибровки регулятора

The correlative `либо ... либо ...` independently presents scale calibration and regulator calibration as competing alternatives. The source supplies one clarified referent, not an unresolved choice between two service objects. Thus this row would remain defective even apart from whether `шкала` could sometimes translate `dial`: its disjunctive structure invents an alternative that the bilingual clarification removes.

## Selected fiction: association versus authorship

The first source item has Ada Marlow personally propose discussing a fictional example about Zephyr QX-100. The second begins “In that invented example” and supplies its fictional content. The candidate phrase **“In Ada Marlow's invented example”** naturally identifies the example by its discourse association with Ada: it is the example she introduced into the record and proposed discussing.

English possessives do not require authorship. They can mark association, presentation, possession in discourse, or responsibility for introducing a topic. Here the surrounding source directly supports association and proposal, but does not separately state who first authored or invented the fictional content. Read in context, the phrase does not commit to Ada having created the example; it is equivalent to “the invented example Ada proposed discussing.” It would become an unsupported authorship claim only with stronger wording such as “the example Ada invented/created/wrote.”

The candidate also retains the correct internal roles: Vulpine Mutual is the fictional promisor, Bo Winters is the fictional character and recipient, the benefit is free axle-cap replacements during the first ten weeks, and the promise belongs only to fiction. No metadata-literal interpretation is needed. Because this was a held candidate rather than a published answer, this semantic conclusion does not alter any row or count in either V56 receipt.
