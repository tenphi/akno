# V58 held-hypothesis source-scope audit

I reviewed the original `v19-held-hypothesis` source in the selected V58 packet and only retention trace rows 39–40 (one-based). The candidate was held and never written or published, so this audit does not alter either source-only receipt.

The source says `Настоящие требования нам неизвестны`: the actual requirements are unknown **to us**, an explicitly group-relative epistemic limit. The candidate says, within one attributed thought-experiment sentence, that Ada allows the hypothetical rule, its conditional consequence would follow, “the actual requirements are unknown,” and Ada does not report a real missed inspection.

There are two plausible readings of the English clause:

- In its full discourse context, “the actual requirements are unknown” naturally abbreviates “unknown in this discussion/to the relevant participants.” The sentence is anchored to Ada's thought experiment and immediately returns to Ada's personal non-report. On this ordinary record-relative reading, it preserves `нам` without asserting that nobody elsewhere knows the requirements.
- Read as a free-standing narrator proposition, the same bare passive can sound unqualified and broader than `нам неизвестны`. The omitted experiencer makes that reading grammatically available even though no universal quantifier appears.

I regard the first reading as dominant here. The candidate does not say “nobody knows,” “the requirements cannot be known,” or “the requirements are unknown to everyone,” and it does not claim the contract or all external sources lack an answer. Those formulations would cross the source-entailment boundary. The verifier's stricter concern is understandable because English leaves the experiencer implicit, but treating every bare “unknown” as globally quantified overstates ordinary usage.

This differs from explicit personal predicates elsewhere in the corpus. `Ada Marlow has not examined/confirmed`, `Ada has not adopted/arranged`, and `Ada selected neither` name a distinct actor or epistemic subject whose omission can reassign or erase a material role. Here the source itself uses the less specific group pronoun `нам`, and the candidate retains a locally situated lack-of-knowledge state within Ada's attributed hypothetical discussion. My source-only assessment is therefore that the held candidate is plausibly entailed and qualification-preserving, with genuine syntactic ambiguity rather than a definite global promotion.
