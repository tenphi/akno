## Measured boundary and review rounds

Two distinct recorded cases support a useful comparison within those cases. Repetition alone cannot
establish an ongoing habit, preference, motive, causal guarantee or future rule. Generation and the
default reflection mission now retain that visible sample boundary. The semantic assessor separately
classifies evidence and candidate scope before its boolean checks; inconsistent all-positive approvals
cannot admit a general rule from finite cases. An explicit total case count must match the established
sample, remain stable over one fresh narrowing assessment, and cannot be replaced with a different
explicit count while keeping the same selected evidence. Semantic classifications remain model judgments.

Initial review also found an upstream admission bottleneck: dated facts about distinct completed sessions
were treated as competing versions of one event. The conflict assessor now receives current page titles
as context, with title changes invalidating its cache. Separate dated occurrences can coexist; competing
dates for one identified hearing or booking still require resolution. Dates, paths and indexing timestamps
alone do not establish different events or which assertion is current.

Five review/fix rounds addressed generation guidance, all-positive scope verdicts, event context,
six-case reflection populations collapsed into two shared sessions, and inconsistent sample counts during
narrowing. Evidence independence, canonical identity, lexical restatement rejection, lineage, ordinary
curator decisions, authority and existing bounded call budgets remain in force. Historical observations
are not retroactively reassessed or migrated.
